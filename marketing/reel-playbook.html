ANGLE A Doomscroll / detox audience

problem-first · reads as help, never as an ad
A1

Relatable confession → practical fixes

Open with an embarrassing scrolling moment, then demo 3–4 fixes on screen. Why it works: confession makes it personal, not preachy; demos make it saveable.

Make it Konvo

“I opened Instagram to reply to one message. That was two hours ago.” → the trap is that your DMs live inside the feed → 3 fixes, last one: “so I deleted the app and kept the messages” + screen recording of Konvo DMs working.

A2

Replacement list, activity on camera

Numbered list of scroll-replacements while journaling / cooking / walking on camera. Why it works: fills the empty habit loop most detox content ignores.

Make it Konvo

“5 things I replaced the feed with — and the one part of Instagram I kept.” Items 1–4 offline; item 5: my actual friends (DMs via Konvo, shown working).

A3

“What it does to your brain” explainer

Talking head + one simple mechanism + captions/diagram. Why it works: provocative question → self-recognition → the mechanism satisfies curiosity.

Make it Konvo

Explain the DM trap mechanically: you open the app with intent (a message), and the feed is one thumb-slip away — variable reward does the rest. “The fix isn't willpower, it's removing the slot machine from the mailbox.”

A4

Relatable skit → phone-setting tutorial

Dramatize the failure, hard-cut to the setting that solves it. Why it works: recognition + humor earns the attention the tutorial converts.

Make it Konvo

Skit: friend sends a Reel at 11pm → you reply → 40 minutes gone. Cut: “here's my phone now” — Instagram gone, Konvo notification comes in, reply, phone down.

A5

Repetitive command / extreme personal rule

Blunt repeated interruption, or an unusually strict personal rule. Why it works: extreme-but-specific beats “use your phone less”; strong curiosity gap.

Make it Konvo

“I haven't opened Instagram in 30 days. I also haven't missed a single message. Both of those are true.” Then show how.

ANGLE B Build-in-public founder

talking head · the app appears as evidence, never as the pitch
B1

Relatable frustration → “so I built it”

30–65s · LightReel: best fit for Konvo

The origin reel — make this first. Personal frustration → why existing options failed → reveal at 5–12s → one unmistakable screen recording → soft CTA.

1 frustration · 2 why existing options failed · 3 “so I built…” at 5–12s · 4 screen recording proof · 5 soft invite

Make it Konvo

Open Instagram to answer one message on camera → get pulled into Reels → “deleting Instagram was never an option, my group chats live there” → reveal: blocked feed and a working DM conversation in the same shot.

B2

Transparent numbers + unresolved update

~60s

Result/number → dashboard receipt in first 5–10s → cause → what's still failing → next experiment. Mixed results with receipts beat pure wins.

Make it Konvo

Weekly real experiments from PostHog: activation rate, screen-time drop, pass usage. “Usage fell X%, but almost nobody disabled DMs” is a stronger story than a download count. Only numbers we can show on screen.

B3

Founder failure → live fix

38–60s

Concrete failure with stakes → show the broken state → fix on camera → lesson or open question. Substantially outperforms routine progress logs.

Make it Konvo — one is live right now

The App Review rejection of build 53 is a ready-made episode: show the rejection, explain the Family Controls entitlement mystery, document the resubmission. Also good: Instagram breaking a selector → the cage-patch hotfix shipping in 60s. Never manufacture failure; film it when it happens.

B4

Objection / question → proof on the phone

40–65s

Comment full-screen → one-sentence answer → demonstrate on a real device → external receipt (privacy label, settings) → invite the next question. Working for the direct competitor: ~154K views.

Make it Konvo — priority questions

Can I still send/receive DMs? · Can I open a Reel someone sends without falling into the feed? · Do you see my Instagram login or messages? (answer with the privacy label on screen) · What stops me from bypassing the block? · Do notifications and group chats still work?

B5

High-stakes serialized challenge

30–50s / episode

Numbered series with a real deadline/stake, scoreboard every episode. Generic “Day 14 of building my app” is weak — the stake must matter outside the product.

Make it Konvo

Stake reflects the mission, not a coding streak: “30 days to cut my Instagram time without missing a single important DM.” Scoreboard: screen time, DMs answered, accidental opens.

B6

Build / demo with a surprising constraint

25–50s

Constraint → fast montage → working product in hand → one lesson. More watchable than a feature announcement because the build itself has a finish line.

Make it Konvo

Reserve for visible ships — closing a loophole a user found, shipping DM-only mode in response to one complaint. Skip for routine polish.

The 8 rules that keep it from feeling like an ad

01Founder has a problem before the product has a feature

“I kept losing an hour after checking one message” — never “seamlessly combines blocking with communication.”

02The app appears as evidence, not the opening pitch

Earn attention with a frustration, result, failure or question; the UI proves the claim.

03There is a visible receipt

Screen Time, working DMs, privacy label, the rejection email, the actual bug. No unsupported superlatives.

04Include something unresolved

A perfectly resolved testimonial sounds scripted. “It works, but people found this loophole” creates a journey.

05Admit trade-offs and wrong assumptions

On camera. Feels like participation in the build, not targeting by a brand.

06Founder language, not company language

“I built”, “I got this wrong.” Never “we're excited to announce”, “revolutionary”, “seamlessly.”

07One reel, one claim

Prove “DMs still work” OR “shared Reels don't open the feed” OR “my screen time dropped.” Never all at once.

08Soft, story-native CTA

“What loophole should I test next?” / “Follow — screen-time result next week.” Not “link in bio” on repeat.
