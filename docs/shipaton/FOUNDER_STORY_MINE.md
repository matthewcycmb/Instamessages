## Q1 (story area 1, the inciting moment)

Asked: Before July 21, when did keeping Instagram uninstalled, or opening it for one message, last cost you something concrete? Where were you, what were you trying to check, and what happened in the next ten minutes?

Answer: (pending)

Eligibility answers (2026-09-03): entering Next Gen. Under the age of majority: "Yes" (read as yes; guardian consent form due before Sep 30). School email on Devpost: "There's a school email, and it is eligible." Prior public release: none ("I think I released the app mid August"; verified store date is Aug 21, 1.0.0).

Answer to Q1 (verbatim, voice transcription, lightly punctuated):
"I've been always trying to lower screen time, stop doing scrolling because it's shortening my attention span, and I just don't feel healthy and good about it when I just scroll endlessly. So I tried all these screen time apps out there, like Opal, one sec, all that. All they do is give you a specific limit, like a five minute limit where you can unlock the app for five minutes or fifteen minutes, and then you can do whatever you want, scroll or message or whatever. And I would always find myself impulsively using that fifteen minutes just to scroll and ignoring the app completely, as if Opal didn't exist. That's when I realized, what if I just deleted Instagram completely? And I did that, the beginning of the summer. I deleted Instagram completely and didn't have it for a good two weeks, but then I redownloaded it two weeks later and found over fifteen unread messages. That made me realize it was kind of mean for me to leave everyone on read and kind of ghosting them. So I was thinking about a viable solution: I want to check my Instagram messages, but I don't want to be sucked into the Reels, feed or Explore, to the point where when I open an app it just goes straight to my Instagram DMs. I was in Hong Kong, I met up with a friend, and we walked around the entire Hong Kong island for eight hours. Maybe in the afternoon, three PM, I told her the idea, and she said it was a really, really good idea and I should pursue it. Originally I thought it would be impossible to alter the UI of Instagram, but I managed to figure out a way using the web view of Instagram and injecting scripts. I still have the original insane brain dump of exactly what I wanted. The first version was basically utilizing Instagram's API, which didn't work because there were so many weird restrictions. The UI was not an Instagram UI and it was really bad. You could only send a message if someone sent you a message, so you can't reach out. So I decided to pivot two days later into an Instagram wrapper. I began building the wrapper on my laptop, realized it was possible, the laptop version was really good, and then expanded to mobile."

Specific and valuable: Opal and one sec named; the exact failure of timed unlocks (the unlock opens everything, so the 15 minutes went to scrolling "as if Opal didn't exist"); deletion for about two weeks; reinstall with 15+ unread; the stake is social (leaving friends on read), not screen time; the Hong Kong walk with a friend, about 3 PM, first outside validation; the belief it was impossible to alter Instagram's UI; the API first version and its "can only reply, cannot reach out" failure (verified: 24 hour reply window and no initiation in the Jul 21 memory; pivot Jul 23, commit f58d1c5); laptop first, then phone (verified: Mac wrapper preceded iOS in git); a surviving brain dump document (unverified until produced).
Vague: dates of the deletion and reinstall; which of the 15 messages mattered and what was missed; what happened in the ten minutes after the reinstall (did the feed take over?); how long he used Opal or one sec and whether he paid; the HK date relative to Jul 21.
Verified vs remembered: API pivot and laptop-first order are verified in git; Opal, deletion, the 15 messages and the HK walk are remembered only. Store release date corrected to Aug 21 by App Store Connect.

## Q5 (story area 4, failure with judgment)
Asked: which failure he got wrong longest, what he believed, the moment he saw the evidence; candidates from the record listed.
Answer: (pending)
Answer to Q5 (verbatim, voice transcription, lightly punctuated): "A lot of people were cancelling the trial because I put a Screen Time setting in the onboarding, and it requires so much commitment, so I decided to move it to the end, actually optional, because I want to convert as much as possible and I don't want people to freak out, oh my god my Instagram app is blocked, and then delete and cancel the subscription immediately. Another thing was the plans didn't load, 'Loading your plans'. The big problem is, after my viral LinkedIn post, a lot of people couldn't access the paywall because of 'loading your plans' or some sort of bug when it's trying to convert the currency, and that really hurt because I lost maybe potential buyers. But now I fixed it. Mostly those two. At the end of the day you just work hard and fix it. Oh, the App Store rejection too. So weird: I require an Instagram testing account, and there's only one section to put the username and password, and no section where they can check the verification code. So they would reject it, and two days later ask where the verification is, and then I wait two days more. Four days for an update I could have done in two. That's why I've been using expedited review, especially for bug fixes."
Specific and verifiable: (a) the paywall outage hit during the Aug 31 spike, which he attributes to a LinkedIn post (new fact: the 280-person day came from LinkedIn; post not yet linked). The record: build 78, 46 people at the paywall, 1 plan tap, 0 completions; a user emailed about "Loading your plans". (b) His belief about the cause ("currency conversion") differs from the verified cause: the paywall gate required three products and konvo.pro.lifetime was never approved in App Store Connect, so the real store never returned it; the dev build read a local StoreKit file that had it, which is why it worked on his phone. Fixed in 1.4.0 (build 82) by requiring only the sold products, with a regression test. This belief-versus-cause gap is the failure arc's centre. (c) The block: he believes moving it out of onboarding fixed cancels; the Sep 1 evening record contradicts him (build 84 without the block still cancelled 11 of 19, versus 16 of 26 with it). The honest arc: removed the block, cancels stayed, found cancels cluster at 0 to 10 minutes because the trial is the only door to the inbox, then shipped the reminder promise, the notifications page and the post-purchase chain in 1.5.0. Sep 2 (build 101 day): 9 trials, 2 cancelled within the hour (small sample). (d) App Review: the 2FA code field gap and four-day cycles, now expedited review. Verified: one 2.1 rejection in the Aug record.
Vague: the discovery moment for each (who emailed, what he saw); the LinkedIn post (date, link, wording); "now I fixed it" without the cause.
